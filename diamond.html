Diamond

“By the end of this tranche, CHIRP must be able to access well-structured data from the 2 existing and up to 6 additional jurisdictions’ reports via an LLM (our own or a 3rd party) to compare incidents, identify recurring patterns and synthesise lessons with conclusions traceable to the underlying source reports.”

CHIRP’s requirements

  • Reports from the UK, the US and up to 6 more countries
  • CHIRP’s own reports
  • Asked through an AI, CHIRP’s own or a third party’s
  • Compare incidents, find recurring patterns and draw lessons
  • Every conclusion traceable to its source report
  • SHIELD matching
  • Kept up to date automatically
  • A validated test set
  • Logging
  • Stretch: open to people CHIRP shares the link with

Glossary

  • Connector. A small new service that users add to the Claude harness. It holds no data: it turns the AI’s requests into calls to CHIRPdb, which does the searching and counting. Users sign in with a CHIRPdb account before it works. This page uses the Claude harness in its diagrams and examples, but the connector is a standard MCP server, so it also works with other AI chat services that support MCP, such as ChatGPT.
  • Shared vocabulary. Each country describes the same thing in its own words: one says “fishing vessel”, another “fishing”, another “trawler”; severity and accident type vary in the same way. We map every country’s terms onto one list for each field, building on the IMO classifications we already use for accident type and severity. We also capture more fields from every report, so a report from any country carries close to the detail the MAIB spreadsheets give. The result: ask about fishing vessel groundings and the answer draws on the matching reports from every country, with counts that compare like with like.
  • Review tool. The part of the CHIRPdb frontend that CHIRP staff use to check reports.
  • Fast ingestion. 2.5 days per country instead of 5. Only some of each country’s reporting rules are built in, so the data is less reliable.
  • Validated test set. A fixed list of questions with known right answers, re-run after every change to catch answers getting worse.
  • Pattern finding and learning from user questions. Asking what keeps recurring across all reports, for example what keeps causing enclosed-space deaths, and seeing the supporting cases. Included in Diamond Finding what recurs across every report. Claude uses a CHIRPdb tool that searches every report in the database, both its metadata and its plain text, not just a handful of top matches. Claude decides what to look for, for example enclosed-space deaths by SHIELD code, vessel type and country, or a phrase such as “double shift” in the text, then compares the results and writes up the lessons. Counts from the metadata are exact, so every number traces back to its reports. Not included in Diamond Patterns in the plain text, and learning. Patterns that appear only in the text are harder: the system has to read the reports to spot them. For example, many reports may mention that the crew had just finished a double shift, but no field records it, so Diamond cannot count it. With learning, the system would spot it in the text and save “crew had just worked a double shift” against each report it applies to, in effect a new piece of metadata. The next time anyone asks something similar, the system checks the metadata first, then the saved findings, and only then goes back to the plain text, so answers get faster and more consistent with every question. Diamond logs every question, which is the starting point for this.
  • Causal AI. Working out which factors actually cause accidents, not just which ones appear together. CHIRP’s experts propose a cause, for example that fatigue leads to groundings, and the system tests it against the reports, showing how strong the effect is; nothing is cited until CHIRP signs it off. It also tests whether a regulation or recommendation worked. Diamond shows what recurs, not what causes what.
  • Log. A record of each question asked and which reports the AI used to answer it.

Questions Diamond can answer

  • “How many fishing vessels have sunk in the last ten years, and in which countries?”
    How Claude asks CHIRPdb count_reports(vessel_type="fishing vessel", vessel_sank=true, accident_date_from="2016-01-01", group_by="country") In plain English Count the reports about fishing vessels that sank since 2016, split by country.
    A typical answer 112 fishing vessels sank across the 8 countries since 2016. The UK had the most (38), then the US (27) and Norway (14). Most capsized after flooding or losing stability. Each count links to the reports behind it.
  • “What keeps causing deaths in enclosed spaces?”
    How Claude asks CHIRPdb search_text(text="enclosed space", deaths_min=1) count_reports(text="enclosed space", deaths_min=1, group_by="shield_code") In plain English Find passages that mention enclosed spaces in reports where someone died, then count those reports by SHIELD code.
    A typical answer Across 46 fatal enclosed-space accidents, two factors recur most: poor planning of the task (29 reports) and procedures not followed (24). A common sequence: the atmosphere was not tested, and a second person died going in to help. Each point links to the report page it comes from.
  • “Compare collisions in the UK and the US since 2015.”
    How Claude asks CHIRPdb count_reports(accident_type="collision", country=["UK", "US"], accident_date_from="2015-01-01", group_by=["country", "vessel_type"]) In plain English Count collisions in UK and US reports since 2015, split by country and vessel type.
    A typical answer A table of collisions by country and vessel type, 212 in UK reports and 184 in US reports, with the biggest differences called out: fishing vessels feature far more often in UK reports, towing vessels in US reports.
  • “Has anything like this happened before? A bulk carrier grounded at night with one person on watch.”
    How Claude asks CHIRPdb find_similar(description="bulk carrier grounded at night, single watchkeeper") In plain English Find past incidents that read most like this description.
    A typical answer 6 similar groundings, closest first: the bulk carrier Ocean Venture, Australia, 2018, grounded at night with the officer of the watch alone on the bridge; and so on, each with a one-line summary and a link to the report.
  • “Write me a toolbox talk on mooring line injuries, from real cases.”
    How Claude asks CHIRPdb search_text(text="mooring line", injuries_min=1) get_report_pages(report_ids=[…], pages="matching passages") In plain English Find passages about mooring lines in reports where someone was injured, then fetch those report pages.
    A typical answer A one-page toolbox talk in plain English: what went wrong in 14 real cases, the warning signs, and what to do differently, each point linked to the report it comes from.

Illustrative: the tool names and fields are settled during the build, and the figures and vessel names in the answers are made up for illustration.
